GPU-Accelerated Multibody Dynamics with Frictional Contact

A Differential Variational Inequality Solver

01

Overview

GPU dynamics without a tree-topology assumption

Newton DVI is a GPU implementation of a semi-implicit differential variational inequality (DVI) time-stepper for rigid multibody systems with bilateral joints, actuation, joint limits, and frictional contact. Bodies are represented in maximal coordinates, while joints and contacts enter through a common constraint-space operator. Time discretization and a convex relaxation of Coulomb friction yield a cone-constrained quadratic program at each step. A decoupled bilateral–unilateral solver combines GPU-accelerated block-sparse LDLT factorization for bilateral joints with matrix-free projected methods for unilateral limits and contacts.

02

Method

Constraint solution within a time step

The default algorithm uses a decoupled bilateral–unilateral solve. Optional alternating block sweeps update the two constraint blocks and repeat their solves to retain more of their coupling.

Robot learning

Robot learning: videos and results

We evaluate DVI with three contact solvers—projected Jacobi, APGD, and P-SPG-FB—across six tree-structured robot-learning benchmarks, with MJWarp as a reference.
